## Interval history

Mr. Ellis returns for scheduled follow-up after completing radiation to the
prostate bed. He feels well, with no new bone pain, no
urinary changes beyond his usual baseline, and stable energy. A family member attended
and had questions about preparing for the next steps.

## Results reviewed

Ultrasensitive PSA has risen on each of the last several draws. It remains
below 0.20 ng/mL but the direction of travel is clear. Recent routine labs from
Dr. Brooks showed a mildly low hemoglobin and a mildly raised fasting glucose,
neither of which needs action from this clinic.

## Assessment

Rising PSA after prostatectomy and adjuvant radiation. The source of
the rising PSA is not yet known.

## Plan

1. **Imaging.** I recommend a PSMA PET/CT at Riverside Cancer Institute to look
   for the source of the PSA rise. Maria Torres, RN, our nurse navigator, will
   coordinate scheduling and the preparation instructions.
2. **Decision visit.** Once the scan report is available we will meet within two
   weeks to go over the result and the treatment options it points to. No
   treatment decisions will be made before the scan has been read.
3. **Bone health.** Start calcium citrate 500 mg twice daily with meals, to go
   with his vitamin D3. Please keep his current medications otherwise unchanged
   and check with Dr. Brooks before adding any other supplements.
4. **Support.** I am introducing Dana Whitaker, LCSW, an oncology social worker
   at Northgate University Medical Center. She will contact the family about
   caregiver support and travel and parking on scan day.
